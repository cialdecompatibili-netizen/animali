The Labrador Retriever comes from Newfoundland (Canada) and was developed in 19th-century Britain as a retrieving dog for hunters and fishermen. Today it is one of the most common breeds worldwide, and a popular guide and assistance dog.

## Appearance
A sturdy, muscular dog standing 55-62 cm at the shoulder. Males weigh 29-36 kg, females 25-32 kg. The coat is short, dense and water-resistant with an undercoat; accepted colours are black, chocolate and yellow. The thick "otter" tail works as a rudder when swimming.

## Temperament
Friendly, patient and deeply bonded to the family. It is intelligent and eager to please, so it is easy to train. It is not a guard dog: it welcomes strangers too.

## Care and health
It needs at least 1-2 hours of exercise a day and loves water. It gains weight easily, so measure its portions. It sheds heavily, so brush it weekly. Best-known issues are hip and elbow dysplasia and eye conditions: choose breeders who test the parents. Life expectancy is 10-12 years.
